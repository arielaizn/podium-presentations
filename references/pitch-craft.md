# Pitch craft — the thinking a deck needs before it needs design

Podium builds slides; this file is the mind behind them. Use it in step 3 (storyline) and step 4 (notes).
Industry-standard presentation practice, distilled into rules the skill applies on every deck.

## 1. One throughline
Before any slide, write one sentence: *what the room should believe or do when it ends*. Every slide must move toward it; a slide that doesn't, goes (or moves to the appendix). The title of the closing slide is the throughline turned into one ask.

## 2. Pick the delivery plan, then write notes for it
Ask (or infer) how the person will present. The notes change with the plan:
- **Scripted (memorised or read)** — full sentences in the notes, in *spoken* language (short, conversational, the way they'd say it out loud; draft by speaking, not writing). Warn them about the middle stage of memorising, when delivery turns stiff and recited: rehearse past it until the words are theirs again. If they'll read on stage: know the text well enough to keep eye contact, and drop the script for the final line.
- **Talking points (unscripted)** — 3–6 word milestones per slide, plus the bridge to the next slide. Unscripted is not unprepared: the route is planned, only the wording is live.
- **Sent, no presenter (PDF)** — no talk track; the slides carry full sentences (density "detailed").
Choose by prep time and confidence; say which plan the notes follow when delivering.

## 3. Engagement: one or two devices, never all of them
Content alone flattens attention. Plan **1–2 devices per ~30 minutes** (one for a short pitch), placed where attention dips (after a dense stretch, after ~20–30 minutes, after lunch). Using many at once turns a talk into a show. Pick from:
| Device | On a slide / in the notes |
|---|---|
| **Question** | A `statement` slide that asks one question. Live: hands or a nod. Online: a closed choice they can type fast ("A, B or C in the chat?"), never an open essay. Use the answer to decide what to deepen. |
| **Prop / object** | A real thing that makes an abstract point physical. Notes: what to hold up and when to reveal it. |
| **Activity** | A 1–3 minute exercise (stand up, pair up, a quick game) with a one-line bridge back to the point. Best when energy drops. |
| **Video** | A short clip as a pattern break (an `image-text` or full-bleed slide with the clip). Keep it under a minute. |
| **Story** | One concrete case: who, the problem, the turn, the result (the `case` archetype, or a `quote` + `image-text` pair). |
| **Analogy / metaphor** | One comparison that makes the complex obvious; put it in the title of the hardest slide. |
Mark the chosen moments in the speaker notes as `ENGAGE: <device> — <what to do>` so the presenter sees them in Presenter View.

## 4. Marketer's checklist before building
- Audience: who is in the room, what they already believe, what they fear.
- Decision: the one thing they must decide; the closing slide asks for exactly that.
- Order of attack: problem → stakes → proof → the offer → the ask (or a storyline from `storylines.md`).
- Proof beats claims: a number, a case, a quote, a demo. Unverified claims get `VERIFY` in the notes.
- One idea per slide; the title states the idea, not the topic.
